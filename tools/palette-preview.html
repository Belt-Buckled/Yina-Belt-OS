What to look for while comparing

CandidateWhat it does wellWhere it fails
Faded cherryCelebration without party-store brightness. Reads like a stamp or a ticket. Holds at tag size on cream and on black.Goes loud fast if brightened. Must stay desaturated.
Aged marigoldWarmest. Old photographs, birthday light.Weakest legibility of the four. On cream paper at small sizes it starts to disappear.
Ink navyQuiet and adult. Never competes with anything.Carries no celebration. The tag stops feeling like a gift.
Oxblood plumGrown, velvet, definitely not cute.Tips moody. On a five-year-old's favor bag it reads solemn.

The rules that keep it working

The accent is disciplined, not decorative.

Allowed

  • The date stamp
  • Hang tag, favor tag, neck label, enclosure card
  • Packaging tape and stickers
  • Story and post template edges
  • The profile mark

Not allowed

  • Anywhere it sits on the same surface as a design you made for a customer
  • On the artwork itself, ever
  • As a background behind product photography

The reason: you make the color. The brand's job is to be the quiet frame around whatever color the customer chose, and to lose that fight on purpose, every time.

Two honest notes on this page. The handwriting is a placeholder font. The real version uses your handwriting, scanned once, which is the part no competitor can copy. And the wordmark here is set in Jost as a stand-in for the direction, not as a chosen typeface.

The page's own headings and background are document styling and are not part of the brand.

Nothing here is committed. Say which accent and I will record the palette in references/brand-days-like-these.md so every draft uses it.
