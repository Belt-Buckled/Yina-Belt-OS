The pattern underneath the score

They are not competing on quality. They are competing on which business this is.

Grown & Growing wins every scenario about the business as it exists today: apparel, for a mom and her kid, sold by word of mouth to people who need to understand it fast. Every paid order so far is apparel. It is the name for what is proven.

Days Like These wins every scenario about the business as it is described in the plan: favors, decor, Christmas, occasions, digital, and whatever comes after. It is the name for where you said you are going.

So the scorecard does not actually decide this. This decision does: is the next year apparel for moms and kids, or is it occasions?

Pick Grown & Growing if
  • Apparel stays the business and favors stay a side experiment
  • Word of mouth inside the mom circle is the main channel for the next year
  • Being instantly understood matters more than being able to widen later
  • You want the $1,000 as fast as possible, from what already sells
Pick Days Like These if
  • Favors, decor and Christmas are real product lines, not tests
  • You do not want to rename the business in three years
  • You are willing to let the identity and tagline explain what the name does not
  • The brand is the feeling, and the products are just surfaces
The honest recommendation

Days Like These, and it is closer than the score looks.

Three reasons it takes it. Christmas and party favors are both inside your own plan, and Grown & Growing cannot say anything about either one. The Christmas footage from last year and the upcoming party are your content, and one of the finalists is mute on both.

Second, renaming later costs more than explaining now. Its weaknesses, being vague to a stranger and forgettable when spoken, are both fixable with a tagline and a logo. Grown & Growing's weakness, a ceiling at kids' apparel, is not fixable at all.

Third, made by hand for days like these is already a hang tag, an ad, and a closing line. The other name does not hand you that.

The counterargument, stated fairly: 100% of revenue to date is apparel, and 0% is favors. Choosing for the plan over the proof is a bet. If the kids' party in this window does not produce a single favor sale, Grown & Growing was the right answer and this page was wrong.
