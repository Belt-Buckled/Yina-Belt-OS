# Brand Name Options, Round 1

Drafted 2026-08-22. Decision due **Friday 2026-08-28** (Week 1, Priority 1).

## The constraints the name has to survive

1. **It cannot mean "shirts."** Party favors, decor and digital transfer files are all in scope. A name
   built on apparel boxes out two thirds of the business.
2. **It cannot mean "kids."** The proven buyer bought a kids' birthday shirt *and* an adult concert shirt.
   She buys for her child and for herself. A kids-only name cuts the adult half of the same customer.
3. **It cannot mean "Yina personally"** unless that is a deliberate choice. A personal name is honest and
   works fine, but it makes the business harder to hand off or sell later.
4. **It has to be sayable at a birthday party.** Word of mouth inside a mom circle is the current
   distribution channel. If it cannot be repeated out loud once and remembered, it fails.

## Direction A — The maker is the brand

Personal, warm, credible. Easiest to build trust with, hardest to ever separate from.

| Name | Why it works | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| **Made by Yina** | Plain, honest, covers every product line. Says a person made this, which is the actual differentiator against print-on-demand. | Generic construction. Hard to own in search. |
| **Yina & Co.** | Lets the business outgrow one pair of hands later. | "& Co." is crowded. |
| **The Belt Press** | Real craft word (heat press), real surname, works for apparel and favors both. | Reads slightly masculine, slightly industrial. |

## Direction B — The craft is the brand

Grounded in the actual process. Distinctive, but needs explaining once.

| Name | Why it works | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| **Warm Press** | The literal mechanism, and it sounds like a feeling. Covers apparel and anything else pressed. | Could read as a bakery or a publisher. |
| **Press & Party** | Says both halves of the business in three syllables. Apparel and favors, one name. | Locks in "party" harder than she may want. |
| **Second Coat** | What a maker does when the first pass is not good enough. Says care. | Reads as painting, not fabric. |

## Direction C — The occasion is the brand

Widest coverage. Favors, decor, shirts, digital files all fit under an occasion.

| Name | Why it works | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| **Party of Ours** | Warm, a little witty, covers favors, decor and apparel without naming a product. Sounds like something a mom says. | Restaurant association. |
| **The Favor Table** | Concrete image every mom of young kids knows. Instantly explains the favors line. | Narrows toward favors, away from apparel. |
| **Little Occasions** | Covers birthdays, holidays, concerts, everyday. Product-agnostic. | "Little" pulls toward kids only. |

## Direction D — The buyer insight is the brand

Built on the actual discovery: one buyer, two occasions, buying for her kid and for herself.

| Name | Why it works | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| **Ours to Wear** | Names the kid-and-me duality without saying "mommy and me." Apparel-leaning but not exclusive. | Leans apparel, weaker for favors. |
| **Mine & Theirs** | The exact purchase pattern, stated. Memorable, slightly unusual. | Needs a beat to understand. |
| **Both of Us** | Simplest expression of the same idea. Very sayable. | Very common phrase, hard to search. |

## Recommended three to take to availability check

1. **Party of Ours** — widest product coverage, warmest, most repeatable out loud.
2. **Warm Press** — most distinctive, most ownable, says craft without saying shirts.
3. **Made by Yina** — safest, fastest, zero explanation needed. The fallback that always works.

## Before committing, check all three

- Instagram handle, Facebook page name, Etsy shop name
- Domain, .com if possible
- USPTO TESS search for a live mark in apparel and party goods classes
- Plain Google search for a local business already using it

**None of these have been checked for availability.** They are creative options only. Availability and
trademark screening is the next step, and it is the step that kills most first-choice names.

# Round 2 — built on contemporary nostalgia

Added 2026-08-22, after the positioning landed. **Round 2 supersedes Round 1.** Round 1 was built on
product coverage. It is technically correct and emotionally empty. Keep it only as a fallback.

## What the name has to carry now

The customer is a millennial mom. She bought a concert shirt for an artist she loved when she was young,
and a birthday shirt for her toddler, from the same maker. Two eras, one order. The name should hold
**then and now at the same time**, without picking a side and without naming a product.

## Direction E — Two eras, one thing

The duality stated directly. Strongest fit to the actual insight.

| Name | Why it works | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| **Both Eras** | Says the whole positioning in two words. Her era, her kid's era, same order. Modern, clean, very sayable. | "Eras" is culturally loaded right now (Eras Tour). May read as trend-chasing, may age. |
| **Then & Theirs** | Her *then*, their *now*. Compresses the entire insight into three syllables and a pun that rewards a second read. | Needs one beat to land. Easy to mishear as "Then and There's." |
| **My Era, Their Era** | Unmistakable. Explains itself with zero setup. | Long. Awkward as a handle. |

## Direction F — Record-store nostalgia

The concert shirt is the origin story, so the vocabulary of music and reissues fits natively. Also gives
a ready-made visual language: liner notes, track lists, tour dates, pressing plants.

| Name | Why it works | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| **The Reissue** | A reissue is *an old favorite, pressed again for today*. That is contemporary nostalgia, literally, and "pressed" is also what she physically does. Covers apparel, favors and decor without naming any of them. | Slightly cool and adult. Needs warmth in the visual identity. |
| **Side B** | B-sides are the deep cuts real fans know. Flatters the customer. Also reads as *her side and their side*. | Short names are hard to own in search. |
| **Encore** | You go back to what you loved, years later, on purpose. Exactly the concert-shirt behavior. | Common word, likely crowded. |
| **Liner Notes** | The part of the record that told you who made it, by hand. Says craft and says nostalgia at once. | More sophisticated than the buyer may need. |

## Direction G — Nostalgia plus the press

Marries the feeling to the physical craft. Most literal, least ambiguous.

| Name | Why it works | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| **Nostalgia Press** | Says the feeling and the method in two words. Zero explanation required. Extends to any product. | On the nose. Less room to grow into something surprising. |
| **Warm Press** | Carried over from Round 1, and it survives the reframe. The heat press, and the feeling of warmth toward a memory. | Does not signal nostalgia on its own. Identity has to do that work. |

## Direction H — The words you still know

Emotional, not clever. Aimed straight at the feeling of hearing your song again while your kid is in the
back seat.

| Name | Why it works | Watch out |
|---|---|---|
| **Still Know the Words** | The most emotionally accurate name on this list. Every millennial parent understands it instantly. | Long. Hard as a handle. Better as a tagline than a name. |
| **Back Seat Encore** | Her music, her kid in the car. Very specific, very ownable. | Narrow. Reads apparel-and-music, weaker for favors. |

## Recommended three, Round 2

1. **The Reissue** — best single expression of contemporary nostalgia, and "pressed again" is literally
   her craft. Covers every product line. Strongest long-term equity.
2. **Then & Theirs** — the buyer insight compressed. Rewards a second read, works for apparel and favors
   equally, and no one else will have it.
3. **Both Eras** — clearest and most immediately understood. The safe modern pick, with trend risk.

**Tagline candidate regardless of which name wins:** *Still know the words.*

## Availability check, still not done

Nothing here has been screened. Check IG handle, Facebook page, Etsy shop, domain, and USPTO in apparel
and party goods classes before committing to any of them.

# Round 3 — built on Yina, not just on the market

Added 2026-08-22, after `references/about-yina.md` landed. **Round 3 supersedes Rounds 1 and 2.**

Round 1 was organized around product coverage. Round 2 added the two-eras insight. Both were missing the
person making the thing, which turns out to be the whole brand.

## What the character note changed

Two lines reframed it:

> *"puts a ridiculous amount of thought into making things special"*
> *"the feeling of an experience matters just as much as how it looks"*

So the business is not custom apparel, and it is not party goods. It is **objects that mark a day, made by
someone who takes the day seriously.** The shirt, the favor, the decor and the digital file are all the
same act: making a specific day for a specific person feel like it counted.

That covers every product line without naming one, it holds both eras without picking a side, and it is
defensible. A competitor can copy a design. They cannot copy caring this much.

## Scoring criteria

## The top three, in full

### 1. Days Like These — 27

The phrase does something almost no other candidate does: it is **simultaneously past and present tense in
feeling.** You say "days like these" about a memory, and you say it about a day you are living. That is
contemporary nostalgia in three words, without the word nostalgia.

- Covers everything. A birthday, a concert, a Christmas party, an ordinary Tuesday.
- Sentimental without being saccharine, which matches "sentimental underneath all that competence."
- Reads naturally on a shirt, a favor tag, a banner and a listing title.
- **Weakness:** common phrase, so search and handles will be a fight. Likely needs a qualifier.
- **Tagline pairing:** *Still know the words.*

### 2. Made to Keep — 26

A promise and a feeling in the same three words. **Made to keep** says this is not disposable, it is built
to last, and it also says this is a keepsake, something you hold onto after the day is over.

- Directly answers the disposable-party-goods and print-on-demand comparison. It is a quality claim.
- The keepsake reading carries all the nostalgia without any era-specific reference, so it never dates and
  it never leans on someone else's trademark.
- Extends naturally: Made to Keep, kept since 2026. Sub-lines write themselves.
- **Weakness:** does not signal the two-eras idea on its own. The identity has to carry that.
- **Tagline pairing:** *A ridiculous amount of thought.*

### 3. The Reissue — 26

Held over from Round 2 because it survives the reframe. A reissue is an old favorite, pressed again for
today, and **pressed is literally the craft.**

- Strongest built-in visual language of any candidate: liner notes, track lists, tour dates, pressing
  plants, catalog numbers. The design system builds itself.
- Best long-term equity and the most ownable of the three.
- **Weakness:** cool and adult. Needs real warmth in the identity or it drifts away from the kids' side of
  the business. Also the most likely of the three to invite artist-reference designs, which is exactly the
  licensing exposure logged in `decisions/log.md`.

## Taglines worth keeping regardless of the name

- **Still know the words.** — the millennial mom line. Instant recognition.
- **A ridiculous amount of thought.** — lifted from the character note. It is the actual differentiator,
  stated plainly, and it is funny enough to be repeated.
- **Made for the day. Kept after.** — explains the whole business in six words.

## Still not checked

No availability or trademark screening has been done on any name in any round. **Days Like These** and
**Made to Keep** are common phrases and are the most likely to be taken. **The Reissue** and
**Then & Theirs** are the most likely to be free.

### 1. Days Like These — 27

Two readings at once, and both are the brand. *"Remember this?"* is the nostalgia trigger, the sound of

### 4. Made to Keep — 26

A kid's birthday is a little day that is enormous to the people in it. Names the exact emotional size of

**Risk:** abstract. Needs the identity to supply the warmth and the subject.

### 7. Took the Time — 24

What a custom gift actually communicates: somebody took the time. That is the entire value proposition of
handmade over bought, stated in three words.
**Risk:** past tense makes it slightly awkward as a shop name.

### 8. Grown & Growing — 24

Her and her kid in one phrase. She is grown, they are growing, both are being dressed by the same hands.
Holds the two eras cleanly.
**Risk:** reads apparel-first, weaker for favors and decor.

### 9. One of One — 23

Every piece is made once, for one person. It is literally true, it separates her from print-on-demand and
from reselling, and it borrows credibility from how collectors talk.
**Risk:** streetwear-coded and a little cold for a mom audience.

### 10. Fussed Over — 23

What she does to every piece, and what the recipient feels. The character note in two words.
**Risk:** carries no era signal at all. Charming but narrow.

## The real shortlist

Three tied at 27, and they split cleanly by strategy:

- **Sentimental Goods** if the priority is owning something nobody else has.
- **Remember This** if the priority is the two-eras feeling.
- **Days Like These** if the priority is warmth and instant likeability.

**Little Big Days** is the pick if the business is going to lean kids and parties over adult apparel.

### 11. The Keeping Box — 28

The highest score across all twenty. A keeping box is where the things you cannot throw away live: the

### 12. The Thought That Counts — 26

The idiom everyone already knows for gifts, and it happens to be her literal differentiator. The character

### 14. Not Store Bought — 25

The clearest anti-positioning on either list. It names the competition (Target, Amazon, print-on-demand)

### 15. Big Feelings — 25

Doing two jobs at once. It is *the* gentle-parenting phrase for toddler emotions, so every millennial mom

- **Risk:** trendy, tied to a parenting-discourse moment that will pass. Also pulls toward toddlers, away

### 16. Cotton & Confetti — 25

Names both halves of the business by material rather than by product. Cotton is the apparel, confetti is
the party. Pretty, rhythmic, very easy to build a palette around.
- **Risk:** the "X & Y" construction is extremely common in the craft world, so it reads familiar.
- **Strength:** the most immediately legible name here. Nobody has to ask what you sell.

### 17. Shoebox — WARNING, likely blocked

Conceptually excellent: the shoebox is where a generation kept photos, ticket stubs and concert tees. It
would have scored 28.

**Do not pursue without legal review.** *Shoebox* is a long-running Hallmark greeting card brand. That is
an adjacent category — greeting cards, party goods, sentimental paper products — which is exactly where
this business sits. This is the highest trademark risk on either list.

Listed only so it is not re-proposed later by someone who has not checked.

### 18. Mom Made — 24

Literal and warm. A mom made this, and a mom is buying it. Says handmade and says the ICP in two words.
- **Risk:** boxes her into mom-identity permanently, and there are a lot of "mom made" shops.

### 19. Noticed — 23

Doing double duty. The character note opens with "she notices everything," and a custom gift is how you
make someone feel noticed.
- **Risk:** abstract on its own. Carries no product, occasion or era signal.

### 20. The Long Way — 23

She does it the long way on purpose. Sourcing compared properly, details fussed over, nothing rushed.
- **Risk:** could read as slow or inefficient rather than careful.

## What was actually verified, and what was not

| Check | Status | Method |

| Instagram handle | **NOT verified** | Instagram blocks automated checks |
| Etsy shop name | **NOT verified** | Etsy returns 403 to automated requests |

## One trademark data point found

**"IT'S THE THOUGHT THAT COUNTS"** was a registered US mark (Serial 77841234, Reg. 3976375), filed 2009,
registered 2011, and **cancelled in December 2021** under Section 8. It covered advertising, marketing and
graphic design services, which is a different class from apparel and party goods.

Read: that specific mark is dead and was never in her classes. It is not a blocker. It is also not
clearance, since it says nothing about other live marks.
