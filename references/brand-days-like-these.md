# Days Like These — brand rules

Chosen 2026-08-22. These are operating rules, not suggestions. Follow them on anything customer-facing.
Full reasoning and the gap analysis live in `tools/brand-foundation.html`.

**Scope note:** this file covers the verbal brand only. Colors, fonts and logo are deliberately not
decided yet.

---

## The four known weaknesses, and the rule that closes each

The name lost four of ten scenarios in the head-to-head. Each loss has a fix.

| Where it lost | Why | The rule that closes it |
|---|---|---|
| A kids' birthday shirt listing | Brand-first titles make an upcoming party sound wistful | **Product first, brand last** on any marketplace listing |
| Said out loud at a party | Three common words, easy to forget, hard to search | **The handoff line.** One fixed sentence she teaches customers |
| The Instagram bio | The name explains nothing in the first two seconds | **The lockup.** The name never appears bare in a cold context |
| A stranger seeing it cold | Could be a candle shop, a podcast, a photographer | **The descriptor** travels with the name, always |

---

## 1. The lockup

The name never travels alone anywhere a stranger might meet it. It always carries the descriptor.

```
DAYS LIKE THESE
Custom shirts, favors and decor. Made by hand, one at a time.
```

**Short descriptor**, when space is tight: `Custom shirts, favors and decor.`

**Bare name is allowed only** where the audience already knows the brand: a hang tag on a delivered
order, a repeat customer's DM, a reply in her own comments.

---

## 2. Taglines

**Primary: *Made for days like these.***

Use it as the default. It is deliberately present and future tense, which is what fixes the birthday
problem. *Remember* would point backward at a party that has not happened yet. *Made for* points at the
day coming up, and still works years later.

**Supporting lines, used by context:**

- ***Still know the words.*** — nostalgia and adult content. Concert shirts, throwback posts.
- ***A ridiculous amount of thought.*** — process and behind-the-scenes content. Sourcing, pressing,
  the fourth revision nobody asked for.
- ***Made by hand for days like these.*** — the hang tag. Long form of the primary.

Never stack two taglines in one place.

---

## 3. The handoff line

Word of mouth in a mom circle is currently the entire distribution channel, so the sentence a customer
repeats matters more than any ad. Teach them this one:

> **"It's called Days Like These. She makes custom shirts and party stuff."**

Fifteen words, no jargon, contains the name and the category. Say it this way every time so it gets
repeated this way.

When someone mishears it, the recovery line is: **"Days Like These, like the phrase."**

---

## 4. Listing title formula

On Facebook Marketplace, Etsy, or anywhere a stranger is searching for a product and not a brand:

```
[Product] + [key detail] + | Days Like These
```

- `Custom Birthday Shirt, 5T, Any Name | Days Like These`
- `Party Favor Bags, Set of 12, Personalized | Days Like These`
- `Custom Concert Tee, Adult 3X | Days Like These`

**Rule: product first, brand last.** Nobody is searching for the brand yet. Brand-first titles are for
later, and for channels where the audience already follows her.

---

## 5. Collections are occasions

The name is about occasions, so the product architecture should be too. This is what finally makes the
name explain the business instead of the other way around.

- **Days Like These · Birthdays**
- **Days Like These · Concerts**
- **Days Like These · Christmas**
- **Days Like These · Everyday**

Organize by the day, not by the product. A birthday collection can hold a shirt, a favor bag, a banner
and a digital file, which is exactly the point.

## 6. Handles and domain

`dayslikethese.com` is registered and does not resolve. Do not wait on it.

- **Verified available:** `dayslikethesegoods.com`, `dayslikethesestudio.com`
- **Handle preference order:** `@dayslikethese` → `@dayslikethese.co` → `@shopdayslikethese` →
  `@dayslikethesegoods`
- **Rule:** whatever is chosen, use the identical handle on Instagram, Facebook and Etsy. Consistency
  beats getting the perfect one.

## 8. What the brand is not

Say none of these, ever:

- **Not "merch."** Merch is bought. This is made.
- **Not "print on demand."** A machine did not decide anything here.
- **Not "cute."** Cute is small. Milestones are not small.
- **Not exclusively kids.** The same buyer buys for her kid and for herself. Copy that speaks only to
  toddler parties cuts half the customer.
- **Not reselling.** Out of scope, permanently.

---

## 9. The licensing line

The brand evokes an era. It does not reproduce an era's logos. See the licensing decision in
`decisions/log.md`. Nostalgia is the feeling, not the trademark.
